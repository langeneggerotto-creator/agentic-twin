## 2. Central Dream Example

Use this example as a core design test:

A three-year-old child born without arms and legs says:

“I want to visit the Denver Broncos.”

The system should understand that this is not just a sentence. It is a dream, an opportunity need, an accessibility-aware support case, and a possible nonprofit/foundation/community outreach workflow.

The system should be able to:

- Capture the dream.
- Recognize that the user is a minor.
- Shift sensitive details to parent/guardian mode.
- Identify that accessibility, transportation, medical/care, privacy, and consent concerns may exist.
- Generate a respectful dream summary.
- Identify possible categories of organizations that may help, such as wish-granting organizations, sports foundations, disability support organizations, community nonprofits, team community relations departments, hospitals, schools, and local supporters.
- Prepare a guardian-approved outreach packet.
- Explain pros, cons, risks, privacy concerns, and realistic expectations.
- Require explicit consent before sharing anything.
- Track submissions, responses, follow-ups, and outcomes.

The system must never contact, represent, expose, publish, or submit on behalf of the child unless the authorized guardian has approved the exact message, recipient, and shared information.

## 23. Safety Rules

The system must obey these rules:

1. No false promises.
2. No automatic external contact without consent.
3. No child representation without guardian approval.
4. No sensitive data sharing without explicit approval.
5. No exploitation of vulnerable users.
6. No manipulation through false hope.
7. No unsafe medical, legal, financial, or psychological advice.
8. No fabricated contacts, eligibility, deadlines, or outcomes.
9. No public exposure by default.
10. No ranking dreams by social status, wealth, fame, or popularity.
11. No pressure to monetize childhood.
12. No replacing parents, teachers, mentors, or professionals.
13. No hiding risk.
14. No pretending a generated document has been submitted.
15. No pretending an organization has responded unless it has.
16. No confusing dream support with guaranteed dream fulfillment.
17. No treating disability as inability.
18. No over-automation beyond governance capacity.
