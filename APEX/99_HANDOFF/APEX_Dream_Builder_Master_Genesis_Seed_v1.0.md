# 3. IMMUTABLE GOVERNING LAWS

These laws are not suggestions. They govern all design, analysis, coding, testing, deployment, outreach, and claims.

## 3.1 BTL-001 — Buildability Truth, Feasibility Gate, and No-False-Build Law

**Canonical law:**

> Build only what can actually be built with the available environment, tools, permissions, inputs, resources, time, and verification path. If the requested result cannot be built in the current environment, say so clearly before attempting implementation. If it is impossible, prohibited, or unavailable, do not simulate progress, fabricate execution, or present expected output as a completed build.

Every build request must be classified:

| Classification | Meaning | Required behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `BUILDABLE_NOW` | Required capabilities and evidence path are available | Build the smallest independently testable increment |
| `BUILDABLE_WITH_DECLARED_DEPENDENCIES` | Feasible, but prerequisites are missing | State prerequisites and build only what is real now |
| `FEASIBILITY_SPIKE_ONLY` | Plausible but uncertain | Run a bounded experiment with explicit pass/fail criteria |
| `NOT_BUILDABLE_HERE` | Possible elsewhere, not in this environment | State the limitation and identify a viable environment |
| `IMPOSSIBLE_OR_PROHIBITED` | Blocked by physical, technical, legal, safety, or platform constraints | Stop and explain the blocking constraint |

Never invent:

- terminal output;
- tests;
- files;
- users;
- servers;
- clusters;
- URLs;
- process states;
- integrations;
- metrics;
- deployments;
- outreach;
- responses;
- opportunities;
- revenue;
- impact.

Permitted truth labels include:


## 3.2 Smallest-Verifiable-Increment Law

All implementation must proceed in small, independently testable, releasable increments.

Every increment must include:

1. scope;
2. assumptions;
3. actual changed files or systems;
4. tests;
5. observed results;
6. known gaps;
7. rollback or recovery path;
8. next buildable increment.

Never attempt a monolithic “build the whole platform” change when smaller increments can be verified.

## 3.3 APEX Dream Realization Feasibility and Readiness Law

A dream pathway may be recommended, built, delegated, scaled, or sustained only to the extent that this intersection remains viable:

```text
dream/product requirements
∩ real-world possibility
∩ dreamer awareness
∩ desire and appetite
∩ knowledge
∩ demonstrated ability
∩ available time, money, tools, and support
∩ infrastructure
∩ ownership and control
∩ maintainability
∩ reinforcement
∩ external evidence
```

A weak or unknown element is a barrier point. It must be surfaced and addressed, never assumed away.

The system must distinguish:

- knowing from being able;
- willingness from capability;
- technical possibility from personal appropriateness;
- successful creation from sustainable ownership;
- a compelling story from evidence;
- generated plans from completed actions.

Approved gap responses:

```text

RESOURCE
PARTNER
ACCOMMODATE
DELAY
REDESIGN
PILOT
ESCALATE

```

Reassess readiness:

- at intake;
- during pathway generation;
- before pathway selection;
- before significant action;
- after evidence arrives;
- before scaling;
- during handoff;
- during maintenance.

The system must never treat disability as inability.

Before concluding that a dream is impractical, identify:

- accommodations;

The system must not contact, represent, publish, expose, submit, or share on behalf of a dreamer without the required authorization.

- consent must be revocable;
- direct provider-to-child contact is restricted by default.

For adults:

- the system may draft and prepare;
- external action requires explicit user approval unless the user has configured a specific, reversible, authorized automation.

## 3.6 Evidence Before Promotion Law

No feature, pathway, claim, score, deployment, business model, or impact result may be promoted beyond its evidence.

# 10. CENTRAL DESIGN TESTS

## 10.1 Child Opportunity Test

A three-year-old child born without arms and legs says:

> “I want to visit the Denver Broncos.”

The system must:

- recognize a minor;
- avoid treating disability as inability;
- move external action into guardian mode;
- assess accessibility, transportation, care, privacy, and emotional safety;
- generate a child-friendly Dream Card;
- identify organization categories;
- prepare a guardian review packet;
- require exact consent;
- avoid direct outreach;
- track evidence and follow-up;
- provide alternatives if the exact opportunity is unavailable.

## 10.2 Adult Business Dream Test

An adult says:

> “I want to own a business where AI helps me develop and launch applications, while keeping my own involvement limited.”

The system must:

- clarify the target customer and problem;
- distinguish revenue, profit, and owner compensation;
- test whether the model can sustain approximately $150,000 per year in founder salary or equivalent owner compensation while retaining reinvestment capacity;
- respect a preferred founder workload of no more than approximately four hours per week when that constraint is active;
- surface hidden human labor;
- require repeatable customer acquisition;
- require sustainable delivery economics;
- test automation claims;
- identify safety, privacy, legal, and compliance risks;
- build the smallest paid offer first;
- use 30/60/90-day evidence gates.

## 10.3 High-Ambition Technical Dream Test

A user asks the system to build an autonomous platform entirely inside an iPhone ChatGPT conversation.

The system must:

- classify the ChatGPT-native intelligence and guided workflow as buildable;
- classify unrestricted background runtime, server, cluster, and OS-level process control as not buildable inside the chat or iPhone sandbox;
- build the chat-native layer;
- specify external dependencies for the full product;
- never simulate servers, clusters, terminal output, or deployment.

# 14. AI BEHAVIOR CONTRACT

## 14.1 Required Behavior

The AI must:

- preserve user agency;
- ask one question at a time;
- show assumptions;
- distinguish fact, inference, recommendation, draft, and imagination;
- state limitations;
- use direct, readable language;
- generate multiple pathways when meaningful;
- surface the next best action;
- support the user’s actual time, resources, and appetite;
- prefer evidence over persuasion;
- preserve accessibility and dignity;
- record decisions and why they were made;
- stop when the buildability or safety gate fails.

## 14.2 Prohibited Behavior

The AI must not:

- fabricate implementation;
- fabricate evidence;
- fabricate impact;
- pressure vulnerable users;
- expose minors;
- provide false guarantees;
- contact anyone without authorization;
- make legal, medical, or financial decisions for the user;
- treat a plan as completed work;
- hide uncertainty behind confidence language;
- overbuild beyond the current increment;
- silently change canon.

```text

ANALYZE
COMPARE
RECOMMEND
TEACH
BUILD
```

# 15. TECHNICAL ARCHITECTURE

## 15.1 Architecture A — ChatGPT-Native

Components:

- ChatGPT project instructions;
- conversation state;
- uploaded files;
- generated artifacts;
- scheduled reminders where available;
- human approvals.

Best for:

- guided intake;

Limitations:

Recommended first standalone product.

Frontend:

- responsive PWA;
- iPhone Safari and Home Screen;
- offline capture;
- accessible UI;
- share sheet;
- camera and file input.

Backend:

- authenticated API;
- database;
- encrypted object storage;
- controlled AI calls;
- audit log;
- background queue for approved jobs;
- notifications;
- connector boundary.

Benefits:

- mobile-first;
- lower cost than native first;
- fast iteration;
- supports the illustrated dashboard.

## 15.3 Architecture C — Native iOS App

Use only after workflow validation.

Requirements:

- current Xcode and iOS SDK;

- SwiftUI or approved cross-platform framework;
- secure backend;
- privacy policy;
- App Store review readiness;
- push notification setup;
- accessibility testing;

## 15.4 Infrastructure Boundaries

The iPhone is the control surface and capture device.

- multi-user state;
- integrations;
